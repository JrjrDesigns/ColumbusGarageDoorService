Why forestry mulching

The smarter way to clear land in Central Ohio

Most property owners in Licking, Muskingum, Knox, and Fairfield counties come to us after getting a quote from someone with a bulldozer. There's nothing wrong with a dozer for the right job — but for most residential lots, agricultural land, and overgrown properties, forestry mulching makes more sense.

The machine grinds everything in place. Trees, brush, invasive shrubs, small saplings — all of it becomes mulch right where it stands. You don't end up with debris piles that need to be hauled out or burned. You don't end up with bare, rutted soil that erodes every time it rains. And you don't end up with a bill for a second pass to deal with stumps.

"We get calls all the time from folks who had someone push everything into a pile at the back corner of their property. A year later the pile's still there, the brush is growing back, and they're asking us to come deal with it. Forestry mulching doesn't leave that problem behind."

Equipment

Tracked mulchers built for Ohio terrain

We run tracked forestry mulching equipment — not skid steers, not brush hogs. Tracked machines handle the terrain in Central Ohio better than wheeled equipment, especially on hillier properties in Muskingum and Knox counties where the ground gets soft after rain.

The mulching head is the business end of the machine. It's a high-speed rotating drum with carbide teeth that grinds trees up to about eight inches in diameter and dense brush down to ground level in a single pass. What's left is a layer of wood chip mulch that breaks down over time and actually improves the soil.

For larger commercial jobs we can bring additional equipment. For residential and agricultural work, one tracked mulcher handles most of what we see in this region.

Common questions

What people ask before they call

What counties do you serve?

Licking, Muskingum, Knox, and Fairfield counties — including Newark, Zanesville, Mount Vernon, Lancaster, and all surrounding townships and rural areas. If you're on the edge of our footprint, call us — we're often flexible.

How much does land clearing cost in Ohio?

Forestry mulching typically runs $1,500 to $5,000 per acre depending on how dense the vegetation is. Brush clearing is $150 to $400 per hour. Fence line clearing runs $3 to $8 per linear foot. We give you a written quote after the on-site visit — no surprises.

What's the difference between forestry mulching and bulldozing?

A bulldozer pushes everything — trees, roots, soil — into piles that have to be hauled out or burned. Forestry mulching grinds vegetation in place and leaves a clean mulch layer. No debris piles, no soil disturbance, no hauling costs. For most residential and agricultural jobs in Ohio, mulching is the better option.

Do I need a permit to clear my land in Ohio?

For most residential lot clearing and agricultural work, no permit is required. Larger commercial projects or work near wetlands or waterways may need permits from Ohio EPA or the Army Corps. We'll let you know at the estimate if anything applies to your property.

How long does a typical job take?

A 2 to 5 acre residential lot typically takes one to three days with our tracked mulcher. Larger properties and denser vegetation take longer. We give you an honest timeline at the estimate — not an optimistic number to win the job.

Is forestry mulching good for invasive species removal?

It's one of the most effective methods for large-scale invasive removal. Bush honeysuckle, multiflora rose, and autumn olive are common problems across Central Ohio woodlots and pastures. The mulcher removes them at scale quickly. For full eradication, follow-up treatment of regrowth is usually needed.
